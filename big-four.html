The Big Four of Dim Sum 点心四大天王

As early as the 1930s and 1940s, Cantonese tea houses were already talking about the "Big Four" of dim sum: har gow, siu mai, char siu bao and egg tarts. Having stood the test of time, these four are still the stars of every Cantonese tea house today.

Har Gow 虾饺

Often called the queen of dim sum, har gow is the signature dish at many restaurants. Its glistening, translucent wrapper hides a generous filling of fresh shrimp. A proper har gow is just the right size to eat in one bite. The wrapper should be as thin as possible, yet strong enough to hold the filling without tearing. Eat it the moment it arrives, while it is still steaming hot, to enjoy the shrimp at its freshest. To judge a chef's skill, count the pleats. Tradition calls for at least seven, and more than ten is the mark of a true master.

Siu Mai 烧卖

On the Cantonese morning tea menu, siu mai is the second brother, ranked just below har gow. To make it, egg, water and flour are mixed and rolled into thin wrappers. A filling of pork, shrimp and shiitake mushroom is placed in the center, and the wrapper is pinched into the shape of a pomegranate blossom. After fifteen minutes over high heat, the soft, delicate wrapper holds a juicy filling of lean and fatty pork, tender on the outside and springy within.

Char Siu Bao 叉烧包

As the name suggests, char siu bao is a bun filled with char siu, Cantonese barbecued pork. The dough is a specially refined leavened dough, and the filling is diced, seasoned pork with a balance of lean and fat. The bun is shaped like a birdcage. As it steams, the top splits open naturally like a blooming flower, revealing the filling as the aroma of barbecued pork drifts out. Unlike an ordinary bun, it pairs the sweetness of the pork with an incredibly soft, fluffy dough, making every bite deeply satisfying.

Egg Tart 蛋挞

The most iconic Cantonese egg tart is the puff pastry version, and making a true one is no easy task. The pastry is folded again and again until it reaches as many as 192 layers. Fresh from the oven, it has a crisp, flaky shell around a sweet, silky custard. The buttery crust crackles with every bite, and the rich taste of egg lingers long after.

Pour the tea, share the baskets. 斟上好茶，共尝点心
